## Replacement / Insertable Prose

Use the following prose edits around the existing equations and proposition blocks. Equations, notation, proposition labels, proof statements, and table references should remain unchanged.

### Before Section 3.3

The assumptions are designed to isolate the economic channel of interest: how ownership reallocates lifecycle cost, service incentives, and recovery responsibility across the OEM, the operator, and the platform. They do not describe every operational detail of a swapping network. Instead, they define a tractable product-service supply chain in which the battery is a circulating asset and service level is an endogenous decision.

### Before Eq. (5)

The segmented demand system is useful for simulation because high-utilization and low-utilization riders respond differently to fixed fees, per-swap prices, and service reliability. For the analytical results, however, the ownership mechanism can be shown more transparently with an aggregate demand function. The aggregate form preserves the two channels that matter for the model: demand decreases with the BaaS expenditure and increases with service level.

### After Eq. (9)

Equations (6)-(9) make ownership operational rather than nominal. The relevant cost is not only the purchase price of the battery. It is the effective lifecycle cost generated by battery stock intensity, financing, degradation, compliance, and residual value. Ownership therefore affects both the level and the marginal cost of service quality.

### Before Section 3.8

The three ownership structures differ in who owns the battery asset, who controls the market-facing service decision, and who captures residual value. The profit functions below keep these differences explicit while using the same demand and service-level primitives. This common structure allows the ownership cases to be compared through lifecycle cost and incentive distortion rather than through separate model assumptions.

### Before Proposition 1

The follower problem identifies the first distortion created by decentralization. Service level increases demand for the whole channel, but the operator invests only according to its retained margin and its own cost exposure. The proposition formalizes when this difference produces service underinvestment relative to the integrated benchmark.

### Before Proposition 5

The coordination problem is multidimensional because BaaS combines a product price, a service price, service-level investment, and battery recovery. A contract that changes only one margin cannot generally align all incentives. The platform-coordinated contract therefore combines revenue sharing, lifecycle-cost sharing, and battery buyback.
