# Style Profile: JEEM Dynamic Writing Skill MVP (Anonymized)

Generated from:

- primary corpus: JEEM papers on environmental economics, carbon pricing, inequality, and welfare;
- optional secondary corpus: field-relevant papers from related journals;
- static base skill: bundled economics base rules.

This profile describes writing patterns only. It does not quote, paraphrase, or reproduce corpus-paper content.

---

## Writing Identity

The JEEM primary corpus reads as economics-first climate-policy writing. The papers typically value:

- a clear policy or welfare-design problem;
- an explicit economic mechanism;
- visible assumptions and model objects;
- benchmark comparison;
- empirical, calibration, or numerical exercises that discipline the argument;
- bounded policy interpretation.

The implied reader is an environmental economist who expects the policy question, welfare criterion, and mechanism to be visible early.

---

## Target-Journal Patterns

| Observed pattern | Corpus role | Public note |
|------------------|-------------|-------------|
| Introductions open from a policy or economic-design problem, then narrow to a mechanism. | primary | Avoid broad climate-policy essays. |
| Literature is used to motivate the mechanism and gap, not to provide a long neutral survey. | primary | A standalone review is acceptable only if it sharpens the gap. |
| Contributions are tied to model structure, welfare criterion, empirical design, or benchmark comparison. | primary | Avoid generic contribution language. |
| Model sections define the environment and policy objects before propositions or numerical work. | primary | Intuition before dense notation helps. |
| Results sections compare policy regimes, mechanisms, or scenarios. | primary | Interpretation should explain why rankings change. |
| Conclusions return to the central policy-design tradeoff. | primary | Do not introduce new claims. |

---

## Secondary-Corpus Patterns

| Observed pattern | Corpus role | How to use |
|------------------|-------------|------------|
| Broader policy motivation can make the opening more accessible. | secondary | Use only briefly, then narrow to JEEM's economics-facing mechanism. |
| Distributional-policy papers distinguish direct burden, recycling, welfare weights, and feasibility. | secondary | Useful for organizing results and discussion. |
| Field-top papers often make the welfare criterion explicit. | secondary | Helpful when the target-journal corpus is mixed. |

Secondary-corpus patterns support the dynamic skill but do not override reviewed JEEM patterns by default.

---

## Cautions

- Do not let policy-accessible secondary papers pull the manuscript away from JEEM's economics-facing tone.
- Do not infer target-journal norms from incomplete PDF conversions.
- Do not turn the literature review into a catalog.
- Do not state implications broader than the model, calibration, or empirical setup supports.
